What the pattern says The baseline was right exactly when the headline signal and the price direction agreed, and wrong whenever a strong headline masked forward or valuation risk. Q1 was a clean beat that went up; Q3 was a clean headline miss into a guidance vacuum that went down. Both were “the number and the reaction point the same way” quarters, and both were called correctly. The two misses share one structure: Q2 was a beat-and-raise whose raise was mostly currency and whose H2 margins were guided lower, and Q4 was a strong full-year wrap whose 2026 growth was decelerating into a large, uncertain acquisition. In both, the print read clearly positive and the stock fell anyway, because the move turned on expectations and the forward setup rather than on the tone of the quarter. Two generalisable findings emerge, and both are directly testable against the LLM arm. 1. On a richly-valued name, the quality of a beat and the level of expectations matter more than the fact of the beat. A 47% EPS jump (Q2) and a raised $51B guide (Q4) both lost money because the bar was already high and the raise or the growth path was lower-quality underneath. The same dynamic capped Q1’s reward to +1.17% despite a 25% EPS beat. Reading the sentiment is the easy part; mapping it to a price reaction is the hard part, and that mapping runs through valuation and expectations. 2. F orward guidance and strategic/governance risk consistently outweighed backward-looking strength. In every quarter the call went wrong, the risk that actually moved the stock — the F/X-driven, lower-quality raise and H2 margin step-down in Q2; the decelerating growth and the WBD integration overhang in Q4 — was visible in the transcript and written into the notes, but was under-weighted relative to the headline EPS and revenue lines. These give the LLM comparison a concrete, falsifiable test. Does the model also get pulled toward the headline beat-and-raise and under-weight the F/X quality of the Q2 raise and the H2 margin step-down? Does it correctly fade the Q4 print on decelerating growth and deal risk, or does it buy the strong wrap the way this baseline did? If the model repeats the human error, that is itself a reportable limitation. If it avoids the error on the two quarters where a careful reader was pulled by the headline, that is a genuine point in favour of LLM-assisted analysis.

DATE

Thursday, July 9, 2026 at 8:15 a.m. ET

CALL PARTICIPANTS

  • Senior Vice President of Investor Relations - Ravi Pamnani
  • Chairman and Chief Executive Officer - Ramon Laguarta
  • Chief Financial Officer - Steve Schmitt

TAKEAWAYS

  • Revenue Growth -- Management reported 7% net revenue growth in the first half of the year, supported by international business acceleration.
  • Global Volume -- Food volumes grew 3% and beverage volumes grew 2% globally, representing the fastest volume expansion for the company since 2022.
  • Earnings Per Share -- Reported EPS increased 6% in the first half while constant currency EPS grew 3% during the same period.
  • International Scale -- The international segment is projected to exceed $40 billion in revenue for the full year, reflecting its growing importance as a profit-accretive driver.
  • International Volume Mix -- International markets now contribute two-thirds of the total company beverage volume and over 50% of total food volume.
  • Permissible Food Portfolio -- Revenue from the permissible food portfolio reached $3 billion and is currently growing at a nearly double-digit rate.
  • Tariff Refund Benefit -- Management expects refund claims for tariffs paid last year to contribute approximately 1 percentage point to full-year EPS growth.
  • PBNA Operating Margin -- PepsiCo Beverages North America operating margin decreased 90 basis points in the second quarter, driven by commercial arrangements with Alani and soft convenience channel performance.
  • International Operating Margin -- Operating margins for the international business expanded by 100 basis points in the second quarter due to improved efficiency.
  • Frito-Lay North America Volume -- PFNA volume remained flat in the second quarter as the company prioritized affordability investments to regain volume share in the category.
  • Advertising and Marketing -- Management plans to increase North America A&M spending in the second half of the year to support top-line growth.
  • Guidance Reaffirmation -- The company reaffirmed its full-year guidance but indicated that results may trend toward the lower end of the provided EPS range.
  • Acquisition Performance -- The company reported that poppi has returned to growth following a distributor transition and that Siete has resolved ingredient supply issues that impacted April and May results.
  • Convenience and Gas Channel -- Softness in the impulse channel was attributed to high fuel prices, which impacted the conversion of store traffic into purchases.
  • U.S. Strategic Growth -- Management identified a long-term revenue growth target for the U.S. business of approximately 3%.
  • Productivity Initiatives -- The company reached record productivity levels in the first half of the year and plans additional layers of cost reduction in the second half to fund growth investments.
  • Energy Segment -- Growth in the energy portfolio was supported by existing partnerships with Celsius and Alani Nu.
  • Logistics Automation -- The company is testing "mixing centers" in the Texoma region to combine food and beverage inventory, aimed at lowering transportation and delivery costs.

RISKS

  • Laguarta stated, "the Iran war and the impact on gas prices has been meaningful, not only in the U.S., but across the world," noting that high fuel costs have slowed the conversion of convenience store traffic into purchases.
  • Schmitt noted that management expects "more pressure on the business from a commodity standpoint" in the second half of the year, particularly affecting the EMEA region.

SUMMARY

Management reported that international markets are increasingly driving total company performance, with the segment expected to reach a $40 billion revenue milestone this year. While North American volumes have been tempered by inflationary pressures and high fuel prices affecting convenience channel traffic, the company is utilizing record productivity and one-time tariff refunds to fund affordability initiatives. The strategic focus remains on portfolio transformation through permissible food offerings and the scaling of recent acquisitions such as poppi and Siete. The company is also testing structural changes in its U.S. logistics to integrate its food and beverage supply chains and lower long-term operating costs.

  • CEO Laguarta noted that the company is "optimizing the return on investment" for pricing actions by tailoring tactics to specific customers and shopping holidays rather than implementing broad price cuts.
  • Management confirmed that the away-from-home business slowed in the second quarter due to supply chain and execution issues but expects acceleration in the third quarter.
  • CEO Laguarta attributed resilient international growth in Europe to the World Cup, stating the event is "helping us to activate the category in a better way" and create new consumption occasions.
  • The company is expanding automation and digitalization across the business to change the cost structure of its North American logistics, specifically in warehousing and fleet delivery.
  • CEO Laguarta stated that the company is "not starving the international business to fund the U.S. business," maintaining that capital and marketing investments remain sufficient for global expansion.
  • CFO Schmitt indicated that fourth-quarter profit performance is expected to be stronger than the third quarter due to the timing of productivity gains and investment cycles.
  • Management observed that while consumer budgets are constrained, the salty snack category continues to grow volume in the U.S., outperforming the broader food space.

INDUSTRY GLOSSARY

  • PFNA: Frito-Lay North America, the company's division focused on salty snacks like chips and dips.
  • PBNA: PepsiCo Beverages North America, the division responsible for the company's beverage portfolio in the U.S. and Canada.
  • Permissible Portfolio: Products designed to meet specific nutritional or dietary trends, such as lower sugar, functional hydration, or portion-controlled packaging.
  • Away From Home: The sales channel encompassing restaurants, stadiums, and other locations where products are consumed outside of the home.
  • Impulse Channels: Retail locations like convenience stores and gas stations where purchases are typically unplanned.
  • A&M: Advertising and marketing expenses.
  • Constant Currency: A financial reporting method that excludes the impact of foreign exchange rate fluctuations to show underlying business performance.

Thursday, Jan. 22, 2026 at 5 p.m. ET

Call participants

  • Chairman and Chief Executive Officer — Lip-Bu Tan
  • Chief Financial Officer — David Zinsner
  • Corporate Vice President, Investor Relations — John Pitzer

Takeaways

  • Revenue -- $13.7 billion, above guidance and representing the fifth consecutive quarter of actuals exceeding forecast, despite ongoing industry-wide supply constraints.
  • Non-GAAP gross margin -- 37.9%, approximately 140 basis points above guidance, primarily boosted by higher revenue and lower inventory reserves, but tempered by more outsourced client products and early Intel 18A ramp.
  • Non-GAAP earnings per share -- $0.15, compared to guidance of $0.08, with the uptick attributable to stronger revenue, gross margin, and disciplined spending.
  • Operating cash flow -- $4.3 billion, enabling positive adjusted free cash flow of $2.2 billion for the quarter after $4 billion in gross CapEx.
  • Full-year revenue -- $52.9 billion, marginally lower year over year due to persistent supply constraints in both internal manufacturing and external suppliers.
  • Full-year non-GAAP gross margin -- 36.7%; a 70 basis point increase, reflecting fewer period charges.
  • Full-year non-GAAP EPS -- $0.42, up $0.55 year over year, benefiting from reduced charges and improved operating leverage.
  • Full-year non-GAAP operating expenses -- $16.5 billion, a 15% decrease, the result of organizational streamlining and reduced complexity.
  • Cash and short-term investments -- $37.4 billion at period-end, bolstered by completed monetizations, government funding, and new investments from SoftBank Group, and NVIDIA.
  • Debt repayment -- $3.7 billion was repaid during the year, improving financial flexibility.
  • Intel products revenue -- $12.9 billion in the quarter, up 2% sequentially, driven by data center strength and increased AI PC unit growth of 16%.
  • Client Computing Group (CCG) revenue -- $8.2 billion; 3 new SKUs for Series 3 (Panther Lake) launched ahead of expectations, with 16% AI PC unit growth, but total CCG revenue down 4% sequentially.
  • Data Center and AI (DCAI) revenue -- $4.7 billion, up 15% sequentially, representing the fastest quarter-over-quarter growth of the decade and exceeding expectations.
  • Custom ASIC business -- More than 50% revenue growth year over year and 26% sequentially, reaching an annualized run rate above $1 billion in Q4.
  • Intel products operating profit -- $3.5 billion, representing 27% of segment revenue, though down about $200 million sequentially due to increased outsourcing and higher seasonal operating expenses.
  • Intel foundry revenue -- $4.5 billion, up 6.4% sequentially; EUV wafer revenue grew rapidly to over 10% of wafer mix in 2025 from less than 1% in 2023.
  • Intel foundry operating loss -- $2.5 billion, which increased $188 million over the prior quarter, linked to the early Intel 18A ramp.
  • All other segment revenue -- $574 million, down 42% sequentially owing to Altera's deconsolidation; primary contributors were Mobileye and IMS.
  • Q1 2026 revenue guidance -- $11.7 billion to $12.7 billion, with a $12.2 billion midpoint reflecting the low end of typical Q1 seasonality; CCG decline expected to outpace DCAI due to supply allocation.
  • Q1 2026 gross margin guidance -- 34.5% on a non-GAAP basis, indicating sequential pressure from revenue mix and increased Intel 18A volumes.
  • Yield improvement rate -- Lip-Bu Tan reported "7%, 8% yield improvement per month," as the company seeks to attain more consistent, industry-standard performance.
  • Intel 18A and 14A process updates -- Steady yield improvement reported for Intel 18A, with 1.0 Process Design Kit (PDK) delivered for 18AP, and active customer engagements for Intel 14A; Intel 14A volume production set for 2028.
  • Advanced packaging progress -- Notable early customer prepayments secured for EMIB and EMIB-T, reflecting tight industry substrate supply and customer commitment.
  • Capital expenditures plan for 2026 -- Guidance shifted from downward to "flat to down slightly," front-loaded to support future demand out to 2027 and beyond.
  • Debt maturities -- Plans to retire all $2.5 billion in maturities as they come due in 2026.
  • Inventory position -- Fixed finished goods inventory has fallen to 40% of peak, necessitating hand-to-mouth supply management, and driving Q1 supply tightness.

Risks

  • David Zinsner stated, "our buffer inventory is depleted," leading to "most acute" internal supply constraints now impacting Q1 revenue and customer support.
  • Lip-Bu Tan acknowledged, "While yields are in line with our internal plans, they are still below what I want them to be. Accelerating yield improvement will be important lever in 2026 as we look to better support our customers."
  • David Zinsner noted that rising prices and tight availability for DRAM, NAND, and substrates "could limit our revenue opportunity this year," particularly for the client market.
  • Gross margin guidance of 34.5% for Q1 2026 is recognized by management as well below targeted levels, with Panther Lake identified as "still dilutive to the corporate average."

Summary

Intel (INTC -3.20%) reported quarterly results above its guidance across revenue, gross margin, and EPS for the fifth consecutive period, despite persistent industry-wide supply constraints. Management confirmed that the supply-demand imbalance is most severe entering Q1 2026 due to depleted inventory buffers, leading to explicit prioritization of server over client shipments. Intel emphasized significant customer engagement and progress in advanced manufacturing processes—specifically Intel 18A and 14A—with volume production of 14A targeted for 2028 and high customer interest in advanced packaging, including prepayments. Internal streamlining actions produced a marked reduction in operating expenses and strengthened the balance sheet via asset monetization, debt repayment, and new partner investments.

  • Management set a flat to slightly down capital expenditure outlook for 2026, shifting emphasis to tool investment to address ongoing capacity bottlenecks, with expectations for supply improvement starting in Q2.
  • Foundry performance saw rapid internal EUV ramp, and early foundry customer indications that advanced packaging revenue may now exceed initial expectations.
  • Intel reported robust growth in custom ASIC and AI-driven servers, while highlighting ongoing efforts to centrally coordinate data center and AI portfolios under new leadership, and to accelerate introduction of multithreading-enabled server products.
  • Gross margin improvement depends on both operational yield gains and product mix, with management explicitly targeting a path to 40% margins as a medium-term goal.

Industry glossary

  • SKUs: Distinct product models or configurations within the Core Ultra Series, representing different performance levels or features.
  • Intel 18A: An advanced semiconductor process node featuring gate-all-around transistors with backside power, claimed as industry-leading and produced in the United States.
  • Intel 14A: Intel’s next-generation foundry manufacturing process, currently in development with targeted volume production in 2028.
  • PDK: Process Design Kit; a comprehensive set of files and tools enabling customers to design chips for advanced semiconductor nodes such as 18A and 14A.
  • EMIB / EMIB-T: Embedded Multi-die Interconnect Bridge technologies; advanced packaging solutions allowing high-density die integration for complex devices, with EMIB-T as a higher-performance variant.
  • CCG: Client Computing Group; Intel’s business segment focused on notebook and desktop CPUs and platforms.
  • DCAI: Data Center and AI; Intel’s business segment serving data center, cloud, networking, and AI infrastructure markets.
  • EUV: Extreme Ultraviolet lithography; next-generation manufacturing technology used for advanced semiconductor wafer production.
  • ASIC: Application-Specific Integrated Circuit; custom-designed chips optimized for targeted workloads such as AI or networking applications.
  • TAM: Total Addressable Market; the total revenue opportunity available for a particular product or business segment.

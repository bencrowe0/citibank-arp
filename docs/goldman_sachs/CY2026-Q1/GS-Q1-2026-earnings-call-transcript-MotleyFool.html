Summary

Goldman Sachs (GS +1.07%) delivered historically high revenue, net earnings, and EPS, driven by record performance in Global Banking & Markets, and robust long-term net inflows in Asset & Wealth Management. The company executed on its strategy of expanding financing activities, particularly in Asia and among ultra-high net worth clients, while maintaining a capital cushion through prudent risk management and dynamic deployment. Strategic acquisitions, notably Innovator, reinforced its competitive position in active ETFs and supported continued diversification of revenue streams. Management articulated a clear focus on leveraging technology, including accelerated investments in AI and cloud migration, to drive efficiency and operating leverage in future periods. The firm’s institutional client base and strong engagement underpinned continued growth in private credit and alternatives fundraising. The company indicated continued support for shareholder returns, and signaled a constructive outlook for activity in M&A and capital markets contingent on macro and geopolitical stability.

  • Denis Coleman specified that approximately $650 million of the $750 million year-over-year increase in non-compensation expenses stemmed from transaction-based expenses, particularly in equities and Asia, with dedicated work streams aimed at managing unit costs.
  • In response to regulatory developments, the firm's CET1 ratio buffer (110 basis points) was recognized as providing capacity for both client-driven growth and shareholder returns.
  • David Solomon stated, "there is no question that with the conflict in the Middle East, IPO activity slowed a little bit, particularly in March," and suggested IPO activity could accelerate with sustained market resilience.
  • The company reported third-party alternatives fundraising totaling $26 billion, supporting its stated annual fundraising expectations and the long-term $750 billion AUS target for alternatives by 2030.
  • Transaction-based expenses, and competitive deposit gathering were flagged as factors weighing on NIM in private banking and lending; management expects this headwind to persist through 2026.
  • Management emphasized progress closing the competitive gap in Asia, indicating more opportunity remains to further expand in the region’s financing businesses.
  • David Solomon attributed durable equity financing revenues to higher market capitalization, increased client engagement, and targeted balance sheet deployment, while cautioning these could reverse under different market conditions.

Industry glossary

  • FICC: Fixed Income, Currency, and Commodities — a division encompassing trading and financing activities in these asset classes.
  • AUS: Assets Under Supervision — total client assets managed or overseen by the firm, including both fee-based and non-fee-based assets.
  • BDC: Business Development Company — a closed-end fund that invests in private equity and debt, often used in private credit platforms.
  • NIM: Net Interest Margin — the difference between interest income generated and interest paid out, relative to interest-earning assets, especially relevant in lending businesses.
  • CET1: Common Equity Tier 1 — a core measure of a bank's capital strength used in regulatory capital requirements.
  • ROTE: Return on Tangible Equity — net income as a percentage of average tangible common equity, representing profitability for shareholders after adjusting for intangible assets.

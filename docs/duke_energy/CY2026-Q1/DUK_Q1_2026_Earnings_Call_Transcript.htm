SUMMARY

Duke Energy Corporation (DUK 1.61%) emphasized a record $103 billion capital plan funded by recent asset monetizations that exceeded $5 billion, supporting continued system investment and customer benefit programs. The company accelerated large load growth with a high-confidence ESA pipeline of 15.4 gigawatts, while about two-thirds of signed agreements are already under construction and structured to protect existing customers through cost-sharing mechanisms. Executives outlined a balanced approach using fixed-rate funding, forward tax credit sales, and ongoing regulatory engagement to maintain rate affordability and capital strength. Merged Carolina utility operations are set for 2027 launch following regulatory approval, positioning the region to realize $2.3 billion in future savings across both territories. Operationally, strategic contract frameworks and industry-leading infrastructure upgrades, including licensed nuclear extensions and AI-enhanced construction monitoring, are advancing as planned.

  • During Q&A, management stated, “we have other tools in our tool bag” beyond tax credits and merger synergies for mitigating customer rate increases in active regulatory cases.
  • Company projects new data center ESA customers may begin taking power as early as second half 2027, with ramping through early to mid-2030s.
  • Savoy disclosed a multiyear forward contract partner for tax credit monetization and expects process continuity as the IRA market matures.
  • Customer savings flowback rates from tax credits will generally be over four years in North Carolina, with company open to accelerating flowback contingent on rate case negotiations.
  • Sideris affirmed ongoing “acceleration in interest” from data center developers in North Carolina, Florida, and southern Indiana regions, citing vertical integration and permitting support as differentiators.
  • Company flagged dynamic load forecast updates, indicating newly signed ESA volumes will be incorporated into upcoming rebuttal and stakeholder discussions.

INDUSTRY GLOSSARY

  • ESA (Electric Service Agreement): Long-term contractual arrangement to provide specified electric capacity to a customer, often with minimum usage and cost-recovery provisions, common for large load data center projects.
  • CWIP (Construction Work in Progress) Rider: Regulatory mechanism allowing partial recovery of construction costs from customers prior to plant completion to limit rate volatility and reduce earnings lag.
  • ATM Program (At-the-Market Program): Equity distribution arrangement allowing shares to be issued incrementally at prevailing market prices, often to meet ongoing capital needs without large single offerings.

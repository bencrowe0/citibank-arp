United Airlines (UAL) - News coverage digest, FQ4 2025 earnings (report date 2026-01-20)

Fetched via web search across free financial outlets. Window: pre-earnings coverage strictly before report_date, no post-earnings content.

Heading into its January 20, 2026 report, United entered the print on a wave of bullish analyst sentiment built over the prior two months, even as bottom-up consensus modeled a year-over-year EPS decline for the quarter itself. On December 2, 2025, TD Cowen reiterated a Buy rating and $125 price target, naming United its "Best Idea for 2026" on expectations that improving Boeing production rates would let United accelerate fleet renewal, driving what the firm called "material tailwinds" on both sides of the P&L - "less spillage, more premium seating" on the revenue side, and gauge growth trimming non-fuel unit costs. That optimism was echoed across the Street heading into year-end: consensus had drifted to a "Moderate Buy" with an average price target near $135.33 after JPMorgan, Citigroup, and UBS all raised their targets in the weeks before the print, and full-year 2026 EPS was already being modeled up roughly 23.9% year-over-year to about $13.01. Against that backdrop, the Q4 2025 numbers themselves looked more mixed: the Zacks/Street consensus modeled adjusted EPS of about $2.97 (down roughly 8.9% from $3.26 a year earlier) on revenue near $15.35 billion, though United's own guidance range of $3.00-$3.50 sat above that consensus figure - a setup where the standalone quarter was expected to soften on tougher comps and lingering government-shutdown-related cost drag from Q4, while the market's real focus was squarely on whether management's initial full-year 2026 guide would validate the bullish fleet-renewal, premium-mix thesis analysts had already priced in.

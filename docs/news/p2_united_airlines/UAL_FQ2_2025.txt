United Airlines (UAL) - News coverage digest, FQ2 2025 earnings (report date 2025-07-16)

Fetched via web search across free financial outlets. Window: pre-earnings coverage strictly before report_date, no post-earnings content.

Heading into its July 16, 2025 report (after the close on a Wednesday), United had recovered materially from the April tariff-driven selloff, but the pre-earnings debate had shifted from "how bad is demand" to "can fare strength outrun a fuel-cost spike." UBS framed the bar as $1.85-$1.90 in adjusted EPS - well above the midpoint of United's own $1.00-$2.00 guidance range - and modeled its own above-Street estimate of $1.91 versus a Street consensus of roughly $1.86, built on assumptions of about 3% capacity growth, a 12.8% rise in unit revenue, and unit costs excluding fuel up around 7%. The wild card flagged repeatedly in pre-earnings coverage was jet fuel: Gulf Coast jet fuel was trading near $3.60/gallon and West Coast fuel near $3.90/gallon in the run-up to the print, well above the $3.30-$3.40/gallon assumption embedded in United's prior guidance, and United typically doesn't update its own fuel-price guidance until the Friday before earnings - leaving the market to guess how much of the spike management would absorb versus pass through. Analysts also flagged that consensus for the following quarter's (Q3) EPS guidance sat around $3-$4, with a higher fuel assumption seen as the key swing factor that could push that range lower. Overall positioning heading into the print was for a likely beat on the reported quarter (premium/international demand had stayed resilient through Q2), with the real uncertainty concentrated in forward guidance and how conservatively management would talk about fuel-cost recovery into the back half of the year.

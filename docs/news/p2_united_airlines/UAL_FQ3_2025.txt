United Airlines (UAL) - News coverage digest, FQ3 2025 earnings (report date 2025-10-15)

Fetched via web search across free financial outlets. Window: pre-earnings coverage strictly before report_date, no post-earnings content.

Heading into its October 15, 2025 report, the dominant overhang was the federal government shutdown that began October 1 and was already in its second week, raising concerns about thinly staffed air traffic control facilities and possible flight disruptions right as the airline entered the print. The market's initial read, however, was fairly sanguine: on Delta's October 9 Q3 call (the sector's first look of the season), management characterized the shutdown's financial impact as modest so far - well under the roughly $1 million/day hit airlines saw during the 2019 shutdown, and a small fraction of projected Q4 revenue - and TD Securities reiterated Buy ratings on both Delta and United, framing any shutdown-related stock weakness as a buying opportunity given the industry's demonstrated operational resilience. Delta and United shares each ticked modestly higher (UAL +1.7%) in the days around that commentary as investors took the "manageable so far" framing at face value. On the numbers, the Zacks Consensus Estimate heading into United's own print sat at $2.64 in adjusted EPS (within United's own $2.25-$2.75 guided range) on revenue of roughly $15.27 billion. Pre-earnings coverage emphasized that most of the quarter's known swing factors - premium/international demand resilience, domestic unit revenue trends, and capacity discipline - were already reasonably well understood from Delta's read-through, leaving the shutdown's trajectory and United's Q4 guidance as the two most consequential unknowns for the print itself.

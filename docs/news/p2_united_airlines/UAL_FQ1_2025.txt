United Airlines (UAL) - News coverage digest, FQ1 2025 earnings (report date 2025-04-15)

Fetched via web search across free financial outlets. Window: pre-earnings coverage strictly before report_date, no post-earnings content.

Heading into its April 15, 2025 report, UAL stock had been hammered by the Trump administration's "Liberation Day" tariff announcement: shares fell roughly 13% on April 3 alone and traded as much as 20%+ below their 52-week high (set earlier in the year around $138) as investors priced in a demand shock to both consumer and corporate travel. On April 10, UAL slid as much as 11.4% intraday, driven in part by a weak read-through from Delta's April 9 print, where management said tariff-driven "uncertainty around global trade" had stalled bookings and cut its implied Q1 revenue growth to roughly 3.3% (from a prior 7-9% guide), while still flagging relative resilience in international, premium, and loyalty-linked revenue - a dynamic investors expected United to echo. Goldman Sachs analyst Catherine O'Brien cut her UAL price target to $92 from $131 on April 8 (reiterating Buy), citing weaker demand tied to rising economic and global uncertainty even as she stayed constructive on the long-term thesis. Heading into the print, Wall Street consensus modeled Q1 2025 EPS of roughly $0.74-0.76 (versus a $0.15 per-share loss a year earlier) on revenue of about $13.23-13.26 billion, implying growth of roughly 6% year-over-year - a deceleration investors were watching closely for signs of tariff-driven demand destruction bleeding into United's domestic and international unit revenue trends. The market's overriding question heading into the print was less about the trailing quarter (largely de-risked by Delta's pre-announcement) and more about whether management would reaffirm, cut, or hedge its full-year 2025 guidance given the macro uncertainty.

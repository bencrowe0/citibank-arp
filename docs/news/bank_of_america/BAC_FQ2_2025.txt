Bank of America (BAC) - News coverage digest, FQ2 2025 earnings (report date 2025-07-16)
Fetched via web search across free financial outlets. Window: pre-earnings coverage strictly before report_date, no post-earnings content.

Ahead of reporting Q2 2025 results before the market open on Wednesday, July 16 - a day after peer JPMorgan Chase reported results and set the table for high expectations across the banking sector - Bank of America looked to extend a streak of beating analyst estimates: revenue beats in five straight quarters (nine of the last ten), and EPS beats in more than ten straight quarters. Analysts expected Q2 revenue of $26.81 billion (up from $25.54 billion a year earlier) and EPS of $0.87 (up from $0.83 a year earlier). BAC shares were down 1.9% to $46.15 the day before the print (52-week range $33.06-$49.30), up 4.6% year-to-date.

Analyst sentiment was mixed and skewing more cautious than prior quarters: HSBC's Saul Martinez downgraded BAC from Buy to Hold (price target raised $47->$51 despite the downgrade), also downgrading JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs, arguing "downside risks" were not fully priced into large-cap US banks given macro uncertainty, slowing growth, and prospective 2025/2026 rate cuts - and suggesting regional banks looked more favorably valued. Baird downgraded from Outperform to Neutral (target $52). Against that, Truist (Buy, target raised $51->$53), RBC Capital (Outperform, target raised $45->$53), and Citigroup (Buy, target raised $50->$54) all stayed constructive. Analysts were also watching how BAC - the third-largest holding in Berkshire Hathaway's portfolio (a $29.2B stake, though Buffett had been trimming it in recent quarters) and the fifth-largest XLF holding at 4.2% of assets - would discuss capital return plans after major banks cleared recent stress tests, potentially opening the door to larger dividends/buybacks.

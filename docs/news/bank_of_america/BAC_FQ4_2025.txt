Bank of America (BAC) - News coverage digest, FQ4 2025 earnings (report date 2026-01-14)
Fetched via web search across free financial outlets. Window: pre-earnings coverage strictly before report_date, no post-earnings content.

Ahead of reporting Q4 2025 results Wednesday before market open (kicking off the financials-sector earnings season), analysts expected BAC revenue of $27.87 billion (up from $25.50 billion a year earlier - a beat streak of 8 of the last 10 quarters, including the just-reported Q3) and EPS of $0.96 (up from $0.82 a year earlier, a beat streak of more than 10 straight quarters). On the Tuesday before BAC's print, JPMorgan had already reported a double beat on strong net interest income, noninterest revenue and Markets revenue, with CEO Jamie Dimon saying "each line of business performed well" - though JPMorgan shares had given back an initial rally to trade down 3.5% on the day. BAC itself had posted 9% YoY net interest income growth (a record) and 13% YoY noninterest income growth in Q3, leading analysts to expect another "double beat" following JPMorgan's lead.

Analyst sentiment was mostly positive heading into the print: TD Cowen (Buy, target raised $64->$66), Goldman Sachs (Buy, $58->$64), Truist Securities (Buy, $58->$62), and Barclays (Overweight, $59->$71) all raised targets, while Wolfe Research was the lone downgrade (Outperform to Peer Perform, no target). BAC stock was down 1.1% to $54.60 the day before the report (52-week range $33.06-$57.55, up 21.2% over the trailing year). Berkshire Hathaway - BAC's third-largest holding at 9.9% of the portfolio (568.07 million shares, ~7.8% of BAC) - remained a closely watched holder under new CEO Greg Abel and Chairman Warren Buffett, with investors watching for further stake reductions given Berkshire's pattern of trimming the position in recent years.

Bank of America (BAC) - News coverage digest, FQ3 2025 earnings (report date 2025-10-15)
Fetched via web search across free financial outlets. Window: pre-earnings coverage strictly before report_date, no post-earnings content.

Ahead of reporting Q3 2025 results Wednesday before market open, Bank of America was one of several banks reporting that week (JPMorgan, Citigroup and Goldman Sachs on Tuesday; BAC, PNC and Morgan Stanley on Wednesday). Analysts expected Q3 EPS of $0.95 (up from $0.81 a year earlier) and revenue of $27.50 billion (up from $25.49 billion) - continuing a streak of EPS beats in more than ten straight quarters, though BAC had missed revenue estimates in Q2. On the Tuesday before BAC's print, JPMorgan had already reported results beating estimates, with CEO Jamie Dimon calling the economy "resilient" but flagging tariffs, trade uncertainty and geopolitical risk as ongoing threats to sticky inflation; several JPMorgan segments posted 20%+ net income growth, setting a high bar analysts hoped BAC could match given Q2's uneven segment performance (declines in several segments partly offset by NII, trading, and asset-management strength).

Multiple analysts raised price targets ahead of the print: UBS (Buy, $55->$57), Evercore ISI (Outperform, $49->$55), Morgan Stanley (Overweight, $50->$66), and Truist Securities (Buy, $51->$56). BAC stock was up 12.6% year-to-date and up 2.0% to $49.82 the day before the report (52-week range $33.06-$52.88). A notable overhang: Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway - BAC's fourth-largest holding (~10% of the portfolio, a 605.27-million-share, $29.57B stake, ~8.2% of BAC) - had been trimming its position, cutting the stake 7% in Q1 and 4% in Q2 2025, leaving investors watching whether the Q3 report and upcoming 13F filing would reveal further reductions or a change in Buffett's conviction.

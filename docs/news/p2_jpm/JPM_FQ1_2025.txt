JPMorgan Chase (JPM) - News coverage digest, FQ1 2025 earnings (report date 2025-04-11)
Reused verbatim from the production track's existing digest (docs/news/jpm/JPM_FQ1_2025.txt) - same company, same real-world report_date, same free-outlet sourcing already verified once for the production pipeline. Fetched via web search across free financial outlets (CNBC, Yahoo Finance/Zacks). Window: pre-earnings coverage strictly before report_date, no post-earnings content.

Heading into the April 11 report, Wall Street consensus called for JPMorgan EPS of roughly $4.61-$5.46 and revenue near $48.2-$48.6 billion, which analysts noted would represent high-single-digit year-over-year growth on both lines; one widely cited estimate had the consensus EPS figure revised about 3% lower over the prior 30 days. Analysts' key-metric models pointed to net interest income (FTE) near $25.5 billion versus $23.4 billion a year earlier, noninterest revenue around $23.4 billion, and markets estimates of roughly $4.8 billion in equities revenue (up about 25% year-over-year) and $6.8 billion in fixed income (up about 16%). This pre-earnings estimate backdrop built on guidance CFO Jeremy Barnum had given in January alongside Q4 2024 results, when he told reporters he expected full-year 2025 net interest income of about $94 billion.

The dominant theme in pre-earnings coverage was macro uncertainty rather than company-specific concerns. President Trump's tariff announcements triggered sharp market volatility in the days before the report: on April 7 JPMorgan's own economists said they were bracing for a possible recession if the full slate of tariff threats took effect, and on April 9 - two days before JPM reported - CEO Jamie Dimon told Fox Business that a recession was "probably a likely outcome" of the escalating U.S.-China trade war, citing JPMorgan house economists' call for a mild U.S. GDP contraction in 2025. Analysts flagged this volatile geopolitical backdrop as a key swing factor for the print, alongside JPM's premium valuation (forward P/E of roughly 13.5x versus an industry average near 12.8x, and a premium to peers Citigroup and Bank of America).

Ahead of the report, sentiment on the stock itself was mixed-to-cautious rather than bullish: coverage described JPM as more likely to deliver a "modest" quarter after a strong Q4 2024, with commentary advising investors to watch management's comments on the 2025 net interest income trajectory and investment banking outlook during the call before drawing conclusions. Existing shareholders were generally advised to hold given the bank's competitive positioning, while prospective buyers were cautioned about valuation and macro risk.

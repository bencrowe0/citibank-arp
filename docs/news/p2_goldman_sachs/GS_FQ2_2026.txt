Goldman Sachs (GS) - News digest, FQ2 2026 (report date 2026-07-13/07-14). Pre-earnings market-expectations context only, sourced strictly before the print.

Goldman Sachs was scheduled to report second-quarter 2026 results on July 14, 2026, before market open. Heading into the print, GS stock had gained about 21% year-to-date, driven by a rebound in investment banking, higher trading revenue, and a run of solid prior-quarter earnings.

Consensus estimates: Wall Street analysts projected Q2 2026 EPS in a range of roughly $14.10 to $14.51 (one source citing $14.51 specifically, versus $10.91 a year earlier), and revenue consensus centered between $16.0 billion and $16.5 billion (one source citing $16.22 billion specifically), implying year-over-year revenue growth of roughly 13% to 48% depending on the estimate cited. Key areas analysts said they would focus on: investment banking fees, institutional/trading (FICC and Equities) revenue, asset & wealth management fee trends, and any updates on net interest income and the deal/underwriting backlog.

Analyst positioning ahead of the report was constructive. Bank of America analyst Ebrahim Poonawala raised his price target on Goldman to $1,150 (from $1,050) and maintained a Buy rating, noting his belief that all eight major U.S. banks could beat second-quarter earnings estimates, helped by stronger net interest income and wealth management inflows, and that better results could support earnings upgrades for the second half of 2026 and fiscal 2027. Evercore ISI analyst Glenn Schorr raised his price target on Goldman to $1,075 (from $950) and kept an Outperform rating, citing a strong outlook for capital markets - rising stock prices, solid M&A activity, and healthy trading volumes - plus growing AI-driven capital-raising activity supporting investment banks, while noting private markets still faced some challenges. As of the pre-earnings period, GS carried a consensus "Moderate Buy" rating among 13 Wall Street analysts (six Buy, six Hold, one Sell), with an average price target of $1,073.58 implying roughly 2% upside from then-current levels.

Options market positioning ahead of the release implied an expected post-earnings move of about 4.78% in either direction, notably above the stock's average post-earnings move of 2.36% over the prior four quarters - i.e. the market was pricing in an unusually large reaction, reflecting elevated uncertainty/anticipation around the print rather than a settled view.

Sentiment heading into the report was described as "constructive," supported by expectations of continued strength in capital-markets activity, though commentary flagged risk around deal-pipeline execution and equity-market swings as factors that could move fee-generating segments in either direction.

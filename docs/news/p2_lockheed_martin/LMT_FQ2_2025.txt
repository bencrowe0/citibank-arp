Lockheed Martin (LMT) - News coverage digest, FQ2 2025 earnings (report date 2025-07-22)
Fetched via web search across free financial outlets. Window: pre-earnings coverage strictly before report_date, no post-earnings content.

Heading into its July 22, 2025 report (before market open), Lockheed Martin's setup skewed cautious despite an otherwise strong operating backdrop. Shares had lost about 4.1% over the trailing six months, sharply underperforming the Zacks aerospace-defense industry's 21.3% gain and peers RTX (+22.7%) and Huntington Ingalls (+25.3%) over the same stretch, leaving LMT trading at a discount (16.43x forward earnings vs. the industry's 28.02x). The Zacks Consensus Estimate called for Q2 2025 revenue of $18.57 billion (+2.5% y/y) but EPS of just $6.52 (-8.3% y/y from $7.11), with the bottom-line estimate cut a further 1.4% in the trailing 60 days; Lockheed carried a Zacks Rank #4 (Sell) with a negative Earnings ESP of -1.21%, meaning the quantitative model was not predicting a beat this time despite the company having topped consensus in each of the prior four quarters (average surprise +11.75%).

Segment-level estimates pointed to broad-based sales growth: Aeronautics (~$7.34B, +0.9% y/y, driven by higher F-35 production volume), Space (~$3.23B, +1% y/y, lunar-program-driven), Missiles and Fire Control (~$3.34B, +7.8% y/y, on JASSM/LRASM/precision-fires ramp), and Rotary and Mission Systems (~$4.66B, +2.4% y/y, Black Hawk/Sikorsky and Canadian Surface Combatant work). Offsetting that top-line strength, analysts flagged lower equity earnings from United Launch Alliance (fewer-than-expected Q2 launches) and lingering cost overruns/withheld payments tied to the F-35 TR-3 software-upgrade delays (72 jets delivered in May 2025 after months of delay per a Bloomberg report) as bottom-line drags. Investors were watching backlog growth (from continued global defense-spending tailwinds) and management's commentary on program execution risk heading into the print.

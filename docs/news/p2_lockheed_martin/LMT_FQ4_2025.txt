Lockheed Martin (LMT) - News coverage digest, FQ4 2025 earnings (report date 2026-01-29)
Fetched via web search across free financial outlets. Window: pre-earnings coverage strictly before report_date, no post-earnings content.

Heading into its January 29, 2026 report (before market open), Lockheed Martin carried a record $179 billion backlog - fueled by the F-35 Lots 18-19 finalization and historic PAC-3 missile demand - that analysts framed as a cushion against the year's operational turbulence, most notably the $1.6 billion of charges absorbed in Q2 2025 on a classified Aeronautics program and rotary systems. Wall Street consensus (per Benzinga) pegged Q4 EPS at approximately $5.87, but a notable gap had opened between that Street number and management's own guidance: with year-to-date EPS at $15.69, hitting the midpoint of full-year 2025 EPS guidance ($22.25, reaffirmed in October) implied a Q4 print of roughly $6.56 - a potential material upside surprise versus the lower analyst bar. Execution on F-35 deliveries was the key swing factor: Lockheed needed at least 32 aircraft delivered in the quarter to hit its full-year target of 175-190 jets, seen as critical to stabilizing free cash flow (guided to $6.6-$6.8 billion for the year). Sentiment on the 2026 outlook was split, with bulls (e.g., Truist) framing 2025's underperformance as a "clearing event" setting up a stronger 2026.

A separate Zacks consensus (via Finviz, three days before the print) pegged Q4 EPS at $6.05 (-21.1% y/y, revised up 0.4% over the trailing 30 days) on revenue of $19.83 billion (+6.5% y/y). Segment-level estimates called for Aeronautics sales of ~$7.95 billion (-0.7% y/y) but a sharp operating-profit rebound to ~$742.2 million (vs. $434.0 million a year ago, when the classified-program charges hit); Rotary and Mission Systems sales of ~$4.66 billion (+9.4% y/y); Missiles and Fire Control sales of ~$3.93 billion (+15.1% y/y, the fastest-growing segment); and Space sales of ~$3.19 billion (+8.6% y/y).

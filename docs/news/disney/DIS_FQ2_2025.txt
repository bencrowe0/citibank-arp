Walt Disney (DIS) - News coverage digest, FQ2 2025 earnings (report date 2025-05-07)
Fetched via web search across free financial outlets. Window: pre-earnings coverage strictly before report_date, no post-earnings content.

Ahead of reporting Q2 FY2025 results before market open Wednesday, analysts expected Disney revenue of $23.14 billion (up from $22.08 billion a year earlier - a beat streak of 3 straight quarters, 5 of the last 10) but EPS of $1.20, actually down from $1.21 a year earlier. Goldman Sachs' Michael Ng (Buy, $140 target, March note) expected DTC strength from "Mufasa: The Lion King," "Moana 2" and price increases, while flagging that domestic theme-park attendance likely declined in the quarter due to the Easter-holiday shift and natural disasters - though he was "encouraged by growth in summer bookings despite recent softness in select travel datapoints and competition from Epic Universe." Bank of America's Jessica Reif Ehrlich (Buy, $140 target) similarly highlighted Parks reacceleration, DTC profitability and a strong movie slate, while flagging macro uncertainty and Universal's Epic Universe theme park opening (May 22) as risks, alongside cost comps from inflation and new cruise-ship spend.

Key items investors were watching: theme-park segment attention ahead of the Epic Universe opening, where Disney had already begun offering discounts to compete, even as management's guidance called for 6-8% full-year Experiences operating-income growth. Disney+/Hulu subscriber trends were in focus after Q1 ended with 178 million combined Disney+ Core/Hulu subscriptions and 125 million Disney+ Core paid subscribers - some recent softness attributed to promotional-offer expirations rather than core demand. On film, Q2 releases "Captain America: Brave New World" ($200.4M domestic / $415.0M worldwide, seen as underwhelming versus recent Marvel films) and "Snow White" ($86.1M domestic, a box-office disappointment) were both below expectations, though Disney still held 5 of the top 10 grossing films of 2025 to date thanks to a weak competitive slate and 2024 carryovers. Forward-looking catalysts flagged for Q3 included "Thunderbolts" (released just before this report), "Fantastic Four: First Steps" (due July), strong "Andor" Season 2 viewership, and the 2025 NFL Draft. DIS stock traded at $92.16 the day before the report (52-week range $80.10-$118.63), down 16.8% year-to-date and over 20% over the trailing year.

Walt Disney (DIS) - News coverage digest, FQ1 2025 earnings (report date 2025-02-05)
Fetched via web search across free financial outlets. Window: pre-earnings coverage strictly before report_date, no post-earnings content.

Ahead of reporting Q1 FY2025 results before market open Wednesday, analysts expected Disney revenue of $24.6 billion (up from $23.6 billion a year earlier - a beat streak of 2 straight quarters, 5 of the last 10) and EPS of $1.45 (up from $1.22 a year earlier - a beat streak of 6 straight quarters, 8 of the last 10). Goldman Sachs' Michael Ng (Buy, price target raised $137->$139 in January) flagged Sports, DTC and Experiences as the key items to watch, expecting Experiences/Sports EBIT to beat consensus and box-office strength from "Moana 2" and "Mufasa: The Lion King" to lift content sales/licensing EBIT; he modeled flat Disney+ core net adds versus a consensus expecting a 1.0 million decline, and projected +6.5 million Disney+ core net adds for full-year F2025 driven by the post-theatrical "pay-1" window and the password-sharing crackdown. Ng called Disney "a high-quality EPS compounder at an undemanding valuation," noting shares had outperformed the market since the November 2024 Q4 report introduced above-consensus three-year guidance, though flagged the still-unresolved pricing/promotion details for the upcoming ESPN DTC launch as a lingering question mark for future calls.

Rosenblatt's Barton Crockett (Buy, price target raised $122->$135 in December) argued a "northward re-rating" was supported by rising confidence in portfolio durability and reduced concern about linear-TV/streaming-competition exposure, while acknowledging management's guided high-single-digit EPS growth for 2025 (and double-digit for 2026/2027) "appears aggressive" but could prove achievable per new CFO Hugh Johnston's recent commentary; he called DTC streaming "the leading driver of segment profit growth in F2026." Other pre-earnings ratings: Citigroup reinstated Buy ($125 target), Redburn Atlantic upgraded Neutral->Buy (target raised $100->$147), Macquarie maintained Neutral ($110 target). Investors were also watching whether Disney+ net-adds momentum could continue given Netflix's just-reported best-ever quarterly paid-member addition, and whether continued Linear Networks revenue decline would again be offset by DTC and Content Sales/Licensing growth. DIS stock was down 1.06% to $112.83 the day before the report (52-week range $83.91-$123.74, up 17% over the trailing year).

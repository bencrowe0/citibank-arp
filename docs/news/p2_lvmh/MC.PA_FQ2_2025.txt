LVMH (MC.PA) - News coverage digest, FQ2 2025 earnings (report date 2025-07-24)
Fetched via web search across free financial outlets. Window: pre-earnings coverage strictly before report_date, no post-earnings content.

Heading into its H1 2025 report (scheduled for July 23-24, after the Euronext Paris close), sell-side sentiment on LVMH was distinctly cautious, part of a broader "sobering time for luxury sales" as HSBC's Global Luxury Goods equities team put it in a July 2, 2025 note. HSBC forecast LVMH's Q2 organic sales would fall 7% year-over-year - a sharper decline than Q1's 3% organic drop (2% reported) - with negative performance across almost every division except Selective Retailing; the Fashion & Leather Goods division (roughly 48% of group sales and 78% of recurring operating income) was expected to see organic sales down 11% y/y, the segment investors were watching most closely. Per FactSet consensus, first-half group sales were expected at approximately EUR 39.89 billion (-3% organic y/y), with operating income before non-recurring items seen falling to about EUR 9 billion (from EUR 10.65 billion in H1 2024) and net income to roughly EUR 5.9 billion (from EUR 7.3 billion). Analysts attributed the sequential slowdown from Q1's 0.4% sector-wide growth to the "Liberation Day" tariff announcements, which HSBC said had stalled confidence, markets and the U.S. dollar in April, though it expected a partial May/June recovery; a weaker U.S. dollar and the health of the Chinese consumer were also flagged as key swing factors given their outsized weight in LVMH's sales mix. In a separate June 12 note, the same HSBC analysts described luxury-segment growth as forming an "hourglass shape," with beauty and very-high-end/very-accessible goods outperforming while the middle of the market was squeezed - a dynamic LVMH's broad portfolio (spanning entry-level to ultra-high-end) was seen as directly exposed to. By contrast, peers Hermes and Prada were expected to keep growing through the same period, underscoring that the luxury slowdown was viewed as uneven rather than industry-wide.

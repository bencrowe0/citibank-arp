LVMH (MC.PA) - News coverage digest, FQ4 2025 earnings (report date 2026-01-27)
Fetched via web search across free financial outlets. Window: pre-earnings coverage strictly before report_date, no post-earnings content.

Heading into its full-year/Q4 2025 report (scheduled for January 27, 2026), sell-side positioning had turned more cautious than optimistic after a rough 2025 for the luxury sector, but a split was opening between the Street's base case and at least one more constructive house view. Per MT Newswires (via MarketScreener, published January 26, 2026, the morning before the report), Morgan Stanley expected LVMH's Q4 2025 revenue to edge up 0.6% year-over-year - notably more bullish than the broader Visible Alpha consensus, which called for a 1.3% decline (a separate consensus figure cited elsewhere pointed to an even weaker -1.6% base case). The setup followed a difficult year in which LVMH shares had fallen roughly 30% at one point during 2025 and traded near multi-year lows, with 2026 guidance already flagged by CEO Bernard Arnault as unlikely to be "simple" given an "unforeseeable" and "disrupted" global economic and geopolitical backdrop.

As of mid-January 2026, third-party 12-month price targets for LVMH clustered in the mid-EUR500s to mid-EUR600s (Yahoo Finance consensus ~EUR639.50; TipRanks ~EUR652.64; StocksGuide ~EUR648.21), with the stock itself trading around EUR649.65 on January 12 - already near the upper end of most published targets, implying the market had priced in relatively little upside surprise. Analysts attributed the target dispersion to differing assumptions on the pace of any China-led luxury demand recovery, margin trajectory, and currency effects rather than one dominant narrative, with commentary generally framing 2026 as a year of limited near-term catalysts (UBS, Neutral, EUR660 target) rather than renewed growth momentum. The Fashion & Leather Goods division - the key swing factor investors were watching - carried a consensus expectation (cited around the same period) for organic sales to fall roughly 3.3% year-over-year in the quarter.
